Read a sensor over SPI

Task: the microcontroller has to read a value from a sensor next to it on the board. Pick both parts, then follow every step: why SPI, who drives which wire, where the clock comes from, how each bit travels, and what breaks when you get it wrong.

3.1Why SPI and not something else?

3.2Who drives which wire?

SPI has four wires. Three are driven by the microcontroller, one by the sensor. The moving dashes show the direction the signal travels.

3.3Where does the clock (SCK) come from?

The microcontroller has to make SCK itself. It starts at an oscillator, goes through the PLL, is selected as the SPI kernel clock and divided down by the SPI prescaler. Change any setting and watch the frequency follow it to the pin.

3.4SPI mode: when is a bit read?

Two settings decide the timing. CPOL (Clock Polarity): is SCK low or high while idle? CPHA (Clock Phase): is the bit read on the first or the second clock edge? Master and sensor must agree, or they read the line while it is still changing.

Who decides? The STM32 can be set to any of the 4 modes (the CPOL and CPHA settings of its SPI). The sensor decides which modes work: its datasheet lists them. You always set the master to match the sensor. The generic sensor accepts all 4. The BME280 accepts only modes 0 and 3, and the ADXL345 only mode 3.

3.5One clock cycle up close

Before watching a whole byte, zoom into the first bits. Does the master put the data on the wire first, or start the clock first? How long is SCK high, how long low? Where exactly is the bit read? The answers depend on the SPI mode, so try all four.
